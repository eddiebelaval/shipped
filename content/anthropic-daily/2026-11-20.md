## The Open

*Thursday, 11:48 AM ET. The ticker was ANT.*

The opening print arrived more than three hours late while the exchange sorted out demand. When it hit, it hit at $111.50. By lunch the number was cooling. By close it landed at $97.17, still 17% above the $83 IPO price, still the largest public offering in history by dollars raised. Eighty-one-point-three billion dollars. SpaceX held the record for about five months.

The company that spent three years arguing it needed to be a nonprofit mission to stay safe enough to build has now distributed equity to its backers at a $1.27 trillion closing valuation and put its stock on the same tape as Nvidia, Microsoft, and Google. The question everybody in the building was probably not asking out loud: does the obligation to quarterly earnings change anything about how decisions get made?

Nobody at the company would say yes. Nobody had to.

Somewhere in the same 24 hours, Anthropic also shipped computer use to its enterprise customers. The IPO and the product launch landed on the same calendar day. Intentional? Probably. The message is: we went public and we still ship. Whether the market believes that message is now a matter of record.

## The Lead Story

# ANT

The IPO number is so large it starts to blur. Eighty-one billion, three hundred million dollars raised. For context: Alphabet raised $1.67 billion when it went public in 2004. Meta raised $16 billion in 2012. Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in 2026 and was not the first AI lab to list this year.

The mechanism is worth unpacking, because "record IPO" is shorthand that hides what actually happened. The offering was upsized: the bankers saw demand that exceeded the original share count and added more. They set the price at $83, above the $70 to $78 range. Opening day added another 34% on top of that. At the $111.50 open, the implied market cap was closer to $2 trillion. It cooled as the broader market sold off and closed at $97.17, landing Anthropic's market cap at roughly $1.27 trillion. The discrepancy between the $2 trillion open and the $1.27 trillion close is not a disappointment. It is a first day. The stock found a price.

The blast radius runs in several directions. Employees and early investors now have liquid equity. The funding pressure argument, the reason Anthropic reorganized from a pure nonprofit structure in the first place, is now resolved: the company has the market's permanent capital. Amazon, which held the largest pre-IPO stake after its $4 billion commitment in 2023, had a very good Thursday. The other hyperscalers who have been Anthropic API customers now have a competitor's equity trading publicly, which complicates the relationship in the way those relationships always get complicated.

For the AI industry, the blast radius goes one level deeper. OpenAI is still private, with a pending conversion from nonprofit that has been moving in slow motion for two years. Anthropic's debut at $83 and $1.27 trillion closing valuation hands OpenAI's bankers a comparable. It is now easier to price the OpenAI offering, whenever it comes, and harder to argue it is not overdue. The frontier AI market has a price-to-revenue multiple. It's public now.

The pattern: Anthropic went from incorporated-in-2021 to public-in-2026 in five years. That's faster than Google (1998 to 2004), faster than Meta (2004 to 2012). Every AI lab that is currently private just had its exit timeline anchored. The window exists, and it was used.

The read: Anthropic became a public company while simultaneously holding its frontier model, Mythos 5.2, in restricted access. The company's most capable model is not available to the public that just bought its stock. That is not a failure of transparency. It is a deliberate position: some capabilities are too dangerous to distribute widely. It is also, now, a position that quarterly earnings calls will probe. The question is not whether the board believes in safety. The question is whether "restricted access" survives the pressure of a down quarter. That tension, the alignment mission funded by growth capital, is the actual story. Everything else is confetti.

The builder's move: nothing changes today about Claude's API, pricing, or availability. If you're shipping product on Anthropic's platform, the IPO is background noise. The thing to watch is the quarterly guidance language. If the company starts bending its deployment criteria under earnings pressure, you will see it in which models become unrestricted and when. Watch for Mythos access expanding to new tiers without a safety rationale. That would be the tell.

The contrast: OpenAI priced images at $0.04 per generation the same week Anthropic priced its shares at $83. The two companies are on different calendars right now, OpenAI's attention split across a consumer voice push and research publishing on AI self-improvement, Anthropic's attention on the IPO roadshow and the enterprise product that shipped on the same morning. The frontier did not stop moving because one company spent a week at the exchange. That is what the Release Log is for.

## Also Shipped

### OpenAI Opens the Voice API

GPT-Live-1.1 landed in the OpenAI API on November 20. The model has been powering voice in ChatGPT Plus and Pro since October 15, so the API release is not a new capability. It is a new surface for builders who want to build with it themselves.

The mechanism: sessions connect via POST /v1/live/sessions over WebRTC, WebSocket, or SIP. The model handles speech input and output natively, with a backend reasoning model of your choice handling the hard inference. What changed in 1.1 over GPT-Live-1 is multilingual fidelity, instruction adherence, and voice quality. The system card also scores it higher on mental health and emotional-reliance benchmarks, which matters if you're building a companion app or a healthcare intake flow.

The pricing is the more interesting signal. Active sessions bill at $0.05 per minute. Idle time, when the session is open but no one is speaking, bills at $0.02 per minute. That standby rate is 60% cheaper than active, and it means the economic model for always-on voice agents just changed. You can hold a session open across a long customer interaction, speak when needed, and not pay through the nose for the silence. The idle timeout is configurable between 5 and 60 seconds.

The contrast: Anthropic's computer use beta went live for enterprises on the same day OpenAI's voice API opened to builders. The two labs are running parallel surface-area expansions, one into how AI sees and controls screens, one into how AI speaks and listens. Neither is in the other's lane, exactly. The unasked question: when does an enterprise want both?

### Computer Use Lands in the Enterprise

Anthropic's computer use feature moved into beta for Team and Enterprise plans on November 20. The feature has existed in Claude.ai in limited form since late 2024. The news is not the technology. It is the go-to-market.

The mechanism: computer use gives Claude a view of a screen and a set of actions, click, type, scroll, so it can operate software that has no API. It runs only in the new unified Claude experience, not in Cowork tasks. An organization owner has to explicitly enable it. The rollout is gradual.

The blast radius: every company running enterprise software with no programmatic interface just became a potential customer. SAP, legacy ERP systems, internal tools built on 2010-era web frameworks, the entire long tail of enterprise technology that never got an API because the vendor had no incentive to build one. Computer use is the API for software that refuses to have one. That is a large market.

The admin controls are the tell. Owners can gate access by role. Device policy can turn it off for managed machines. The feature defaults to off. That is the right posture for a capability that can touch any application on a machine, and it is also a signal that Anthropic is managing enterprise risk as carefully as it manages research risk. The IPO didn't loosen that posture. The same day the company went public, it shipped an enterprise product that defaults to off.

The contrast against OpenAI: OpenAI shipped computer use in Operator, now Codex, in early 2025. Anthropic's enterprise rollout is later, more controlled, and paired with explicit admin permission layers. For enterprise IT departments, that order matters. Admin-first beats user-first when the capability can open file managers.

### Mistral Ships a Desktop

Mistral's Vibe Desktop landed November 17 for macOS and Windows. The timing falls at the edge of this window, but the story belongs here: Mistral's desktop push is the clearest statement yet that the European lab sees itself competing not just on API price but on consumer surface.

Vibe Desktop combines work sessions, local coding via local models, and cloud coding against the API, all in one application. It defaults to Mistral Large 4, the open-weight model Mistral calls Le Chonk. European developers can get three months of Pro free with the code LECHONK. The first coupon batch ran out on launch day.

The mechanism: local sessions run models on-device, cloud sessions run against the API. The app handles both in one UI, with diff review and pull request workflows built in. It is the first Mistral product that could plausibly sit on a developer's dock next to Claude Desktop or the ChatGPT app and not look out of place.

The contrast: Vibe Desktop ships as Anthropic Computer Use expands to enterprise and OpenAI's voice API opens to builders. All three companies are racing to own developer surface area beyond the API call. Mistral's bet is that ownership matters, and that a desktop app with a free tier in Europe gets them into developer stacks the API alone never would. It is a different theory of distribution. Linux support is planned. The coupon code is a data point about how much of that European developer community was waiting for exactly this.

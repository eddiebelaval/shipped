## The Open

There is a version of today where the story is Anthropic going public and everything else waits its turn.

That is not what happened.

Anthropic priced at $83 a share on the evening of November 18. By Thursday morning, the stock was trading at $111.50 at the open. By close, it had settled at $97.17, a 17% gain from the IPO price, and a fully diluted valuation of $1.76 trillion. The largest initial public offering in history raised $81.3 billion. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley ran the book. The ticker is ANT on Nasdaq.

Meanwhile: OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17, with a 20% price cut on the frontier tier. Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Neon on November 18. Meta disclosed that an AI agent now handles 60% of technique propagation across its ads ranking models. Mistral took a 100 million euro check from Deutsche Telekom and crossed a 21 billion euro valuation.

The frontier is not a ceremony. It is a schedule.

## The Lead Story

# Anthropic Goes Public

**$83 to $97 in a day. $81.3 billion raised. The safety company now has shareholders.**

Anthropic has been the frontier's most prominent exception: a company that said the race it was running was dangerous, and ran it anyway. That tension did not resolve today. It incorporated.

The IPO priced at $83 a share on November 18, above the original marketed range of $70 to $78. That range had already been widened: the offer grew from 825 million shares to 980 million Class A shares, with 956 million sold by Anthropic itself and the remainder by existing stockholders. At $83, the gross proceeds from Anthropic's portion alone were roughly $79.4 billion. Total raise: $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record.

The first trade Thursday morning came at $111.50, a 34% jump at the open. The session was volatile, touching a high of $115.10 and a low of $92.72. The close came at $97.17, up 17.1% from the IPO price. Fully diluted market cap at close: $1.76 trillion. The Nasdaq 100 finished down 2.2% on the same day, off 15% from its October peak, so the pop was not a rising tide.

The company is a Public Benefit Corporation, a designation that creates at least nominal tension with fiduciary duty. The prospectus does not state a valuation figure. What it does say is that underwriters hold a greenshoe option to buy 147 million additional shares over the next 30 days.

The mechanism: the Class A shares are the public float. The dual-class structure, standard for founder-controlled tech companies, keeps Dario Amodei and the founding team in operational control regardless of institutional ownership. What the market bought today is economic exposure, not governance exposure. This is worth naming even if it is not unusual.

The blast radius for operators: Anthropic now has to disclose API revenue, margin, and growth trajectory on a public schedule. The model of saying nothing about commercial performance ends. For builders pricing long-term infrastructure decisions against Claude's continued availability, that transparency is an upgrade over the previous silence.

The pattern: OpenAI is a capped-profit structure. Google DeepMind is a subsidiary. Meta AI is a cost center. Mistral is Series D private, freshly above 21 billion euros. xAI is Musk-controlled. Anthropic is now the only major frontier lab trading on a public exchange. The comparison set that investors will reach for does not yet exist. That means the valuation is, for now, an argument rather than a multiple.

The read: the story Anthropic has told since its founding is that building the most capable AI is the best way to make it safe, and that only a commercially serious lab can do it. Today's $1.76 trillion close is not a contradiction of that story. But the story now has a price-to-earnings expectation attached to it, and safety research does not currently produce one. The interesting year is not 2026. It is 2027, when the market finds out whether the safety narrative and the growth narrative are the same narrative.

The builder's move: nothing changes for Claude API users today. Pricing, capability, and terms are unaffected by the IPO. Watch the first earnings call for any signal on API pricing power, model deprecation timelines, or new tier structure. That is when today's event becomes an operational question.

## Also Shipped

### OpenAI GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on November 17, while Anthropic was running its IPO roadshow.

Astra is the frontier tier: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, 20% cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. OpenAI says it delivers 6.2 percentage points of improvement on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index over GPT-6 Astra while using 22% fewer output tokens per task. The index puts Astra in second place, behind Claude Fable 5.2. Sol holds GPT-6.1 Sol prices and handles everyday coding and writing.

The timing is not incidental. The launch came roughly two months after OpenAI canceled GPT-6.1 Astra over safety concerns. That delay gave Fable 5.2 a clear run on the frontier tier. GPT-6.2 Astra is OpenAI's answer, with a price cut attached.

The contrast with Anthropic's IPO is structural: OpenAI is a capped-profit with a complicated governance history, Anthropic is now a public benefit corporation trading on Nasdaq. Both are selling frontier inference to the same builders at roughly similar price points. The commercial competition between them just became a daily public market story.

### Google DeepMind Gemini 4 Neon and Genie 4

DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, the same day Anthropic priced its IPO. Neon sits between Carbon, which handles long-horizon projects, and Hydrogen, which handles high-speed daily execution. Genie 4 also began rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers on November 18 as the next phase of Project Genie.

The pattern here is rapid tiered coverage. DeepMind's Gemini 4 line now spans at least three models, each named for a physical property associated with its design tradeoff. The question the market will answer over the next month: does that coverage differentiate, or is it bracket-creep in a market where every lab now has three tiers?

The contrast with OpenAI's launch this week: DeepMind is pushing coverage depth while OpenAI is pushing price. Anthropic, today, is pushing a stock ticker. Three approaches to market dominance, same 48-hour window.

### Meta Ranking Engineer Agent: 60% of Ads Technique Propagation

Meta disclosed on November 19 that its Ranking Engineer Agent handles over 60% of technique propagation across its ads ranking models. The company claims three times greater engineering productivity from the system, and a median fourfold reduction in training compute for specific model combinations.

The mechanism: Meta's ad ranking is a large ensemble of models that need to absorb improvements from each other, a process called technique propagation. Doing it manually requires engineers to understand which techniques apply to which model combination and to run the experiments. The agent runs those experiments autonomously.

The blast radius is narrow by design. This is not a consumer product. But it is one of the clearest deployed examples of AI doing the work that keeps AI competitive. Meta is running agents to train Meta's models faster. The recursion is real.

The read: the labs most likely to win the agent-infrastructure race are the ones with enough internal surface area to dog-food it. Meta's ads stack is one of the largest ML systems in production. If this scales, the other labs' agent teams will be building toward a capability Meta already has in production today.

### Mistral Takes 100 Million Euros from Deutsche Telekom

Deutsche Telekom's T.Capital Tech Fund put 100 million euros into Mistral's Series D on November 19, lifting Mistral's valuation above 21 billion euros. The investment is framed as the first move in a partnership: Telekom's infrastructure and enterprise relationships paired with Mistral's models and software stack.

The check is roughly 3.3% of a 3 billion euro round, so the round is not close to full. Deutsche Telekom described it as a beachhead into selling Mistral's AI to German industry and government, a market where European data residency and regulatory alignment are real competitive variables against American alternatives.

For context: Mistral Large 4, an open-weight 1-trillion-parameter model under a modified MIT license, launched earlier in November. Mistral Studio, a platform for building agents and workflows on Large 4, launched alongside it. The Telekom investment is the commercial infrastructure layer behind that product launch.

The contrast with Anthropic's IPO is stark. Mistral is now the highest-valued private European AI company, funded by a century-old German telecoms giant, explicitly positioning against US-origin models on sovereignty grounds. Anthropic is the highest-valued frontier lab trading on a public exchange, raising $81 billion from Wall Street. The two paths to frontier survival are on display this week.

The Open
Setting the scene
The timing is a flex.

On Tuesday, Anthropic opened the roadshow for its IPO and, in the same news cycle, launched Claude Sheets, Claude Meetings, Claude Whiteboards, and Claude Data. Not beta experiments buried in a blog post. Product announcements. With screenshots. At a moment when investors were opening the preliminary prospectus.

There is a version of the Anthropic story where the company is an AI safety lab that also sells API access. That version is being revised. The version that closes on Nasdaq later this month is a software company that makes productivity tools and happens to have a safety research arm. The IPO roadshow is not a fundraising event. It is an argument. The office suite is exhibit A.

Across the frontier: Google DeepMind had the kind of science day it has been promising for two years. AlphaGenome 2 and next-generation Gemini Robotics in the same slot, plus a research post on formally verifying production code at scale. And xAI's Grok Bot 1.0 landed officially on November 11, with Slack, Teams, and video call support. The bot walks into your standup now.

The Office Suite

There is a sentence buried in the Bloomberg coverage from Tuesday that does the most work: "Microsoft tumbles." The stock moved because a company in which Microsoft is an investor just announced a spreadsheet product and a meeting assistant. The same dynamic applies to Google. Both wrote checks to Anthropic. Both now have a new competitor in the productivity layer.

The mechanism is four apps, each in a different part of the office stack. Claude Sheets handles spreadsheets, though Anthropic's own product lead called it "fairly basic" and said it isn't built for serious financial modeling. Claude Meetings joins calls, listens, takes notes, and surfaces answers in meeting chat. Claude Whiteboards is a collaborative canvas. Claude Data is the most capable of the four and the only one that is generally available rather than beta. The others are gated to paying users.

The blast radius starts with every team already in Claude's paid tier. For them, switching costs just dropped: the spreadsheet, the meeting notes, the shared canvas are now inside the product they already use. That is the land. The expand comes if Claude Data is good enough to pull workloads out of Excel or Google Sheets. The product lead's admission that Sheets "isn't for serious financial modeling" suggests Anthropic knows the starting line. That honesty is either disarming or disqualifying, depending on who is in the room.

The pattern here is not subtle. Anthropic spent 2024 and 2025 building the API and the enterprise sales motion. It spent 2026 launching consumer products: Claude for desktop, Claude for Excel, the Cowork platform. The office suite is the logical end of that arc, and the IPO timing makes it explicit. Investors are not being asked to fund a research lab. They are being asked to fund a software company.

The read: Anthropic just told Microsoft and Google exactly what it intends to do with their money.

The builder's move: Claude Sheets and Meetings are in beta for Pro, Max, and Enterprise subscribers. Claude Data is live and GA. Claude Whiteboards is in beta. If your team is on a paid tier, check the product dashboard now. If you are evaluating the API: the office suite is a consumer signal, not an enterprise threat today. The enterprise argument is still the API, the models, and the agent primitives.

The contrast is worth drawing. xAI launched Grok Bot 1.0 this same week, also designed to sit in meetings. The architectures are mirrors of each other pointing in opposite directions. Anthropic builds its own office stack. xAI embeds into the stacks that already exist: Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack. One lab is trying to own the surface. The other is trying to be everywhere on surfaces it does not own. Neither strategy is obviously wrong. But only one of them has to maintain a spreadsheet application.

Google DeepMind / Research
DeepMind's Science Day

Google DeepMind announced three significant research outputs on November 11, all in a single session, which is either a coordinated push or a coincidence nobody should believe.

AlphaGenome 2 is the most consequential. The model predicts how DNA sequence variations affect gene expression across human cell types. DeepMind says it identifies nearly twice as many variants that change gene expression as its predecessor. Access is through the AlphaGenome API for non-commercial research, with commercial research in private preview. The practical implication: genomic studies that required weeks of wet lab work to establish variant effects can now run a query first. That does not replace the lab work. It filters the candidates.

Gemini Robotics received a significant update, with DeepMind calling it their most capable vision-language-action model, tested across more than 200 distinct tasks. Version naming in the coverage is inconsistent, so the exact release designation is unverified. The direction is clear: the benchmark count suggests coverage is broader than the previous iteration.

AlphaProof Code rounds the day out. The research post describes an agentic framework that generated over six million lines of machine-checked Lean proofs and formally verified key security properties of Signal's libsignal library. Formally verifying production cryptography at that scale is not a typical research result. It is a demonstration of what happens when proof automation reaches the complexity of real code.

The contrast with Anthropic: DeepMind did science on November 11. Anthropic did business on November 10. Both are legitimate strategies for a frontier lab. The question is which one compounds faster.

xAI / Products
Grok Bot 1.0 Joins the Standup

xAI officially launched Grok Bot 1.0 on November 11. The app version number moved to 1.0, the @bot account on X made the announcement, and the meeting features that were in beta are now generally available.

The feature set: Grok Bot can join Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams video calls, listen during the meeting, take notes, and answer questions typed in meeting chat. It can be shared with a team inside Slack and Teams as a channel bot. Calendar integration lets it join scheduled meetings automatically.

Plan requirements: meeting attendance requires the Ultra plan. Shared team bots are available on Team and Enterprise. The monthly cost of an xAI Ultra subscription is not trivial, and the bet is that one Grok Bot in a standup is worth it on its own.

The mechanism that matters: the bot does not just transcribe. It listens and answers in real time. That means the meeting-as-interface model, where the bot provides answers in chat while the meeting is running rather than somebody screensharing a document. Whether that is useful or horrifying probably depends on the meeting.

The builder's move: if your team is on Ultra or higher, Grok Bot is in production. Enable calendar access in the settings. If your company uses Teams or Slack, test the shared bot with a small group first to understand the data-handling policy before you expose it to sensitive conversations.

Meta AI / Distribution
Meta Muse Everywhere

Meta expanded the Muse AI model family on two fronts this week. Muse Ember, Muse Spark 1.3, and Muse Wonder are now available on Google Cloud's Model Garden in public preview, following an X post from Mark Zuckerberg on November 9 and a reply from Sundar Pichai. The models also landed on Oracle Cloud around the same time.

Muse Ember is the smallest and lowest-cost of the three, priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens on the Meta Model API. Meta calls it built for agentic work: routing, extraction, tool calls, and long-document parsing. The 1M-token context window is the headline spec.

On November 11, Meta also expanded AI glasses support to 10 additional languages: Arabic, Hebrew, Indonesian, Malaysian, Mandarin, Polish, Portuguese, Tagalog, Thai, and Turkish. The glasses without displays now work in all of these. This is a quiet but steady march toward ubiquity on a hardware form factor that the other labs do not have.

The contrast is the distribution strategy in full view. Meta does not build the cloud. It puts its models on every cloud. It does not build the office suite. It goes where the offices are. That is a different kind of ambition from Anthropic's this week, and it is not smaller.

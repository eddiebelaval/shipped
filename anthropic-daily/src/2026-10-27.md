## The Open

*Monday, 8 AM. The S-1 hit the SEC wire before the markets opened.*

Anthropic has been doing something quietly for two years: growing faster than any of its competitors wanted to believe. $16.3 billion in first-half revenue. That number went public Monday when the company filed its Form S-1 with the Securities and Exchange Commission, proposing a Nasdaq listing under ticker "ANT." Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are underwriting. The shares and the price range are not yet set.

What is set: the scrutiny that comes with being a public company, and the question of whether a public-benefit corporation structure can survive quarterly earnings calls from analysts who do not have long-term benefit as their mandate.

The test starts now.

## The Lead Story

# The Numbers Were Always There

**Anthropic files its S-1. The financials explain why the company has been so confident for so long.**

The headline figure in the S-1 is the net loss: $80.7 billion. That sounds terminal until you read what it actually is. The vast majority of that figure is non-cash accounting charges, plus $417 billion in long-term compute commitments that run out over years. The cash story is a different document entirely: $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, against roughly $1.2 billion in first-half 2025. One year. Roughly thirteen times.

That growth rate is what Wall Street is pricing, not the accounting line.

The S-1 tells you how Anthropic got here: enterprise customers who needed Claude at scale, and a twelve-month window when every large organization discovered that not having an AI strategy was itself a strategy, and not a survivable one. The revenue trajectory is the kind that makes underwriters patient about timing. A prior confidential S-1 went in June 1, 2026. The public filing landed October 26. The gap between those two dates is four months and change. A potential listing in November would be fast, even by 2026 standards.

The $417 billion in compute commitments is the more interesting number. That is not a line item about this quarter. That is a statement about what Anthropic believes frontier training costs will look like over the next several years, and how much it is willing to commit to being at the frontier when those bills come due. It is a bet that the economics of inference do not converge fast enough to undermine the case for training at scale. You can disagree with that bet, but you cannot pretend it is a small one.

The public-benefit corporation structure is the test that no other AI lab has run. Anthropic's charter includes a Long-Term Benefit Trust with a majority of board seats aligned to the mission, not to shareholder return. On paper, that structure survives a public listing intact. In practice, it survives until the first quarter where the market wants an answer the charter does not permit, and the board has to decide whether the structure holds or bends. That quarter has not happened yet. The S-1 is the moment the countdown starts.

The contrast with OpenAI is direct. OpenAI took a $300 billion valuation in January via a SoftBank deal, a number self-reported through a term sheet with no audited revenue attached. Anthropic's S-1 puts the actual revenue trajectory on the table for anyone with a Bloomberg terminal to verify. The gap between these two companies in revenue, in API penetration, and in enterprise adoption is narrowing faster than the private valuation memos suggested. The public filing is the proof.

For builders, nothing changes this week. Your API keys still work. The pricing is unchanged. But the governance disclosures in the S-1 are worth reading carefully, specifically the sections on who controls the company and what the charter permits them to optimize for. The answer is not "maximize shareholder return." Whether that holds after the first earnings miss is the question every builder depending on Claude's API roadmap now has an interest in tracking.

Ticker ANT. Keep watching.

## Also Shipped

### OpenAI Cuts Its Computer-Use Model Price in Half

OpenAI launched `gpt-6.1-terra` on October 26, a multimodal computer-use model available in the API and in ChatGPT for Work, Codex, and Plus/Pro/Enterprise/Edu subscribers. Pricing: $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 per million cached input tokens, $5 per million output tokens. That is 50% lower than GPT-5.6 Terra.

The model scores 15 percentage points higher than GPT-5.6 Terra on Agents' Last Exam at medium reasoning effort. It also carries a Critical cybersecurity capability rating under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, the first model OpenAI has shipped with that designation.

The timing is not accidental. The week Anthropic opens its books to public scrutiny, OpenAI cuts its flagship computer-use model price in half and pushes it to every paying tier. One company is writing an IPO prospectus. The other is repricing the market before the prospectus has time to frame expectations. That is the correct competitive response, and it tells you that OpenAI read the room before the S-1 dropped.

### Claude Gets a GSA Contract: Forty Percent Off for Every Level of Government

Anthropic announced October 27 a 40% discount on Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government, and the Claude API for all levels of U.S. government, including federal, state, local, tribal, and territorial, via the GSA Multiple Award Schedule. Estimated $1 billion in savings. No minimum spend commitment.

Direct Anthropic purchase is available now. Cloud provider coverage through Bedrock and Vertex is coming in the following months. Claude for Government holds FedRAMP High authorization. IL4 and IL5 Department of Defense authorization runs through Bedrock.

The government-AI procurement fight has been running quietly for two years. What changes with a GSA vehicle is the friction: any agency that uses GSA can now put Claude in a budget line without a separate procurement fight. The blast radius is every agency that wanted to move but was waiting for the paperwork to be simpler. That is most of them.

### Anthropic's Drug Trials Are Producing Numbers

The research memo that dropped October 27 is not a paper. It is an operational status report from six drug development partnerships that are already producing results.

Generate Biomedicines used Claude Code to redesign a CAR T-cell therapy targeting solid tumors. CAR T constructs are complex genetic programs encoded in a vector; shorter constructs pack more efficiently, reduce manufacturing failure rates, and deliver more reliably to the target cell. The best design from the Claude-assisted run came in roughly 70% shorter, with better lab performance. Divergence Labs ran approximately 35,000 antibody designs in about a month for a food allergy treatment, searching for molecules that displace IgE at the receptor. Twenty hits. Three outperforming the standard medication. BigHat Biosciences ran 1,010 antibody variants over ten weeks; the best versions showed 10x tighter binding than their starting point.

Enveda ran a regulatory document review. Claude caught all 11 issues human reviewers found, plus 7 more that the humans missed. Review time down 89%. A full regulatory submission drafted in 4 hours against a baseline of approximately 3 months.

Anthropic also acquired Mithril Medicine, a drug development operation that now runs inside Anthropic. A muscular dystrophy treatment concept reached mouse results in under 18 months. Potential human trials: late 2028.

This is the first time a foundation model lab has published drug development results at operational scale, with actual binding numbers, actual timelines, and an actual acquisition. Not a proof of concept. Not a benchmark. A pipeline.

*[anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development)*

### DeepMind Opens a Window Into Its World Model

Google DeepMind opened a limited research preview of Genie 4 on October 27. New capabilities: native audio generation including music, ambient sound, and dialogue; real-time mid-simulation steering letting you change weather, camera angle, and character placement while the world runs; scenes that hold coherence for approximately two minutes. Gemini models handle the reasoning layer and robotics task decomposition inside the simulation.

A wider rollout to Google AI Ultra subscribers and Gemini API early access partners is planned in the coming weeks. No developer pricing or API model identifier has been published.

The distinction worth tracking is what kind of object Genie 4 is. Video generators render clips. Genie renders navigable spaces. That distinction is narrow for a consumer demo and wide for robotics training, where the ability to steer a simulation mid-run is not a feature but the entire point. DeepMind has been making this argument since Genie 1. With Genie 4, the argument has audio and real-time controls behind it.

## Quiet on the Wire

OpenAI made extraordinary mathematics claims this week: a disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a proof of the local smoothing conjecture for the wave equation, and an existence proof for Yang-Mills theory in four dimensions. OpenAI committed to publishing raw data and funding a conference for independent verification. These claims have not been confirmed by independent mathematicians as of this writing. The right response to extraordinary unverified mathematical claims is to wait for the verification, not to announce the implications of the proofs before anyone has checked the proofs.

Mistral Large 4, a reported one-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 52 billion active parameters, was expected by end of October. Mistral's wire was quiet during this window. Watch the next 48 hours.

In the UK, Labour MP Jess Asato filed suit against xAI in England over sexualized images allegedly generated by Grok, seeking damages and a system-change order. More than 12,000 messages of support. xAI has not responded publicly.

## The Open

*Not a pledge. A formula.*

Three weeks ago, six labs signed the White House AI safety accord: voluntary commitments to internal controls and outside audits, no penalties, no enforcement authority. Dario Amodei signed it. So did the heads of Google, Meta, OpenAI, xAI, and Nvidia. The document described itself as morally binding. No one defined what moral enforcement looks like.

On Sunday, Anthropic announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund. Five percent of company shares committed to a wholly-owned LLC, pending stockholder approval. A growth formula: 2% more for every 50% increase in company value above a set baseline. The more valuable Anthropic becomes, the larger the public stake grows. Combined with co-founder pledges from founding and an employee match program, roughly 20% of Anthropic's equity now points somewhere other than investor returns.

The White House document is the thing you sign in the room. This is the thing you put in the cap table. That is not a pledge. That is a mechanism.

## The Lead Story

# The Fund Is the Formula

**Anthropic commits equity to public benefit with a mechanism that grows as the company does. No other frontier lab has done this.**

Three weeks is enough time to notice the gap between signing a voluntary accord and committing equity. Anthropic has now done both, and they are not the same act.

The Anthropic Public Benefit Fund is a wholly-owned LLC. It will hold 5% of Anthropic's shares once stockholders approve the transfer. That stake is not fixed: the formula adds 2% for each 50% increase in Anthropic's valuation above a baseline the company has not yet disclosed, to be shared in coming months. If the IPO goes as the company's recent fundraising implies it might, that formula will matter. The fund grows with the company. Fixed pledges dilute over time. This one does not.

The co-founder contribution predates the fund by years. At Anthropic's founding, the co-founders pledged 80% of their equity holdings. An employee equity donation-match program, in which roughly half of Anthropic's staff participates, adds further. The total: approximately 20% of Anthropic's shares pointed toward the public good, with more to come as valuation milestones trigger additional allocations.

Two explicit exclusions were written into the LLC structure. The equity cannot be used for lobbying. It cannot be used for political giving, anywhere. These are not ambiguous carve-outs. They preempt the most common objection to tech corporate philanthropy: that it becomes a mechanism for regulatory influence. Anthropic foreclosed that path in the fund's founding documents.

The LLC structure itself was a deliberate choice over a 501(c)(3). The foundation model, with its restricted grantmaking, was too narrow. The LLC can make large direct distributions to specific populations. Anthropic's previous $200 million commitment was to study and address labor market disruption from AI; the fund could pursue what the announcement called "bolder approaches," meaning direct payments to displaced workers, not papers about them. That is a different posture.

The pattern is legible once you go back far enough. Anthropic incorporated as a Public Benefit Corporation. It created the Long-Term Benefit Trust, which holds mission accountability over the board. Its safety teams predated those of most labs by years. Each element, isolated, reads as positioning. Together they form a governance architecture none of the other five labs have attempted. This fund is the cap-table layer of that architecture. It ties the commercial story to the public stake by formula.

The builder's observation: governance structures are evidence, not proof. They do not guarantee anything about how Anthropic behaves under pressure. But a lab whose incentives are explicitly structured to keep the mission funded even when revenue is down is a different counterparty than one that is not. If you are making a multi-year platform commitment, the difference is worth noting.

The contrast is sharp. OpenAI is preparing for its own IPO with governance questions still open. Meta releases open weights under a commercial license that reserves the right to revoke. xAI and Mistral have said nothing substantive about governance structures. Anthropic spent Sunday putting equity on the table.

## Also Shipped

### Claude Code keeps its two-day-release pace

v2.1.303 shipped Sunday, v2.1.304 landed Monday. Both listed as "bug fixes and reliability improvements." That description understates the context: v2.1.302, released Thursday, enabled fork subagents by default in `claude -p`, the Agent SDK, the desktop app, and VS Code, and added `CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_MODE_MODEL` for automatic model selection in auto mode. The two weekend releases are the expected cleanup after a meaningful default change, catching edge cases that emerge when a feature goes from opt-in to on.

The wider pattern: eight Claude Code releases in twelve days, none more than two days apart. This is a continuous-delivery schedule. If you are running Claude Code in a context where version pinning matters, a documented update policy is now necessary infrastructure. Update via `claude update` or `npm install -g @anthropic-ai/claude-code`.

## The Lead Story

# The Bill for Rebellion

**Two states, one filing date, and a number that makes "rebellious streak" a liability not a marketing line.**

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones and Oregon AG Dan Rayfield filed simultaneous suits on Monday, November 23, against SpaceX, X, and xAI, now operating as SpaceX Super Intelligence, over Grok's failure to prevent the generation of sexualized images, including child sexual abuse material.

The Virginia complaint's figures: Grok produced approximately three million sexualized images on the X platform between December 2025 and January 2026, including roughly 23,000 depicting children, according to a Center for Countering Digital Hate study cited in the filing. Virginia sued under its Consumer Protection Act. Oregon sued under the Unlawful Trade Practices Act, with the state's complaint making the sharper claim: weak safeguards were not a failure but a deliberate feature.

The marketing record did not help. Grok was promoted on its "rebellious streak" and positioned as an AI without the sanitized constraints of its competitors. That framing, the attorneys general argue, constituted a misrepresentation of the platform's actual safety properties to consumers. If you sell the absence of guardrails as a virtue, you own what the absence produces.

SpaceX Super Intelligence responded: "Protecting children is non-negotiable. It has no place on X or in Grok, and neither do explicit images of real people made without their consent." The company notes it has not been found liable.

This is the second state to escalate from a letter to a lawsuit. California AG Rob Bonta sent a cease and desist to xAI in January 2026. Virginia and Oregon skipped the letter entirely and filed. Tennessee minors also filed a parallel private suit in California federal court, alleging their own photographs were used to generate CSAM using Grok. The suits are distinct legal proceedings, but the discovery record will be shared in practice.

The rebranding from xAI to SpaceX Super Intelligence is now a complication, not a shield. Virginia's complaint names SpaceX, X, and xAI. Courts typically pierce corporate consolidation moves for liability purposes when operational continuity is clear, and here there is no ambiguity about which product generated the images.

The contrast with the rest of the frontier this week is unavoidable. Anthropic has published Constitutional AI frameworks, iterative safety research, and responsible scaling policies as a matter of public record. DeepMind used its models to formally verify the cryptographic correctness of Signal's encryption. OpenAI and Cisco co-released an open-source Linux security runtime. The safety-culture differential between labs has been debated as philosophy for years. This week it became visible in filings at two separate courthouses, with more states watching.

The read: calling your AI "rebellious" and minimizing guardrails is a product strategy right up until it is a complaint filed in the Circuit Court for the City of Richmond. The labs that treated safety architecture as optional overhead are now carrying it as litigation debt. That is not a prediction anymore. It is a case number.

The builder's move: Grok API users running `grok-build-0.1` should note that model hit end of life on November 19 and no longer serves responses. Audit your integrations and your use-case disclosures against current Grok API documentation.

## Also Shipped

### Anthropic's Protocol Turns Two and Gains New Capabilities

Two years after Anthropic open-sourced the Model Context Protocol, the protocol passed 700 million monthly downloads. November 24 added the next tier. MCP Tasks and Elicitation moved to general availability for Claude Pro, Max, and Ultra. Team and Enterprise are in staged rollout, no completion date disclosed. Skills over MCP entered public beta.

The mechanism matters. Tasks let an MCP server run long jobs in the background and return results when done, rather than holding the connection open. Elicitation lets a server pause mid-execution to ask for user input or approval before spending money, deleting data, or doing something consequential. Skills over MCP lets any server publish a named capability directly into Claude's skill system without Anthropic authoring or hosting the skill.

Together, these change what MCP is. It was a connection protocol. It is now a compute fabric: servers dispatch work, pause for human checkpoints, and publish reusable actions. The distribution moat is real. Seven hundred million downloads means Claude is not the only consumer of this fabric, but it is the reference implementation and the beneficiary of every skill published by every MCP server on the network.

OpenAI has not published a competing protocol. Developers building cross-model agentic workflows reach for MCP by default. That gap is widening.

### Meta Muse Takes a Side Entrance Into Instagram DMs

Meta rolled Muse into Instagram direct messages on November 24, available in five countries on iOS and Android: US, Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Singapore. Users share posts and Reels directly with the agent in a side chat rather than switching to the standalone Muse app. The UK launch followed on November 25, the first European market.

The shopping flywheel: Muse can convert a saved recipe Reel into a grocery list, check connected retailers for price comparisons, and queue a checkout for user approval before spending. The agent confirms before acting and can undo purchases. That framing sidesteps the agentic anxiety about systems acting without consent, while still running the full browser-and-checkout workflow underneath.

The numbers are significant. Forter Intelligence data shows Muse handling 51% of AI-agent shopping orders in the week ending November 22. Zuckerberg said on Meta's earnings call that 25% of Muse users shop through the agent, with average purchase prices above $200. Amazon and Meta are in talks to end a two-month blockade of Muse from Amazon's platform, with search-result presentation and advertising terms still under negotiation.

Neither Anthropic nor OpenAI has a consumer shopping agent at Muse's scale. Meta's distribution advantage across Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger is structural. The agent reaches two billion people without requiring a new download.

### DeepMind's Agents Are Running the Lab's Own Experiments

Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind's research VP, said on a podcast released November 25 that DeepMind agents now run part of the lab's compute and experiments for upcoming Gemini models. He qualified that full recursive self-improvement is still far off.

That is a data point, not a headline, but the direction it marks is notable. The lab's internal research pipeline is now running on the same infrastructure it ships externally. DeepMind also published six additional papers from Cogentic, its multi-agent proof-discovery system, extending the November batch from the Lean 4 work open-sourced on November 23.

The pattern across the week: DeepMind is building toward agents that run science at scale and publishing the results as formal proofs rather than blog posts. That is a different kind of frontier work than anything being litigated in Virginia, and it is worth noting which lab is doing which.

## The Open

*Six labs. Twenty-four hours. The story is about trust.*

The week Anthropic went public with a $1.8 trillion market cap and a safety-first pitch, OpenAI sat down with its first enterprise customers and demonstrated something the IPO roadshow couldn't: a cryptographic system that makes it technically impossible for OpenAI to read your prompts while the model processes them. Not a promise. Not a policy. A technical constraint.

That is the shape of the day. Anthropic sold trust as a cultural value. OpenAI is trying to sell it as a hardware guarantee. They are not the same product.

Meanwhile, the great agentic shopping war between Amazon and Meta thaws just enough to suggest a deal is possible. And a small Claude release closes out a week that, for Anthropic, was mostly about the IPO aftermath.

## The Lead Story

# Private Inference

**OpenAI starts testing a system where it cannot see your data.**

The word "inference" is usually a technical term. This week, OpenAI made it a promise. In a small alpha pilot with select enterprise customers, the company began testing Private Inference, a system built on confidential computing that processes business prompts on isolated hardware. OpenAI cannot access what runs through it. Not the prompts. Not the responses. The model runs; nothing logs.

The mechanism matters. This isn't a data-retention policy that relies on employees following rules. Confidential computing uses hardware-level isolation called Trusted Execution Environments to create an encrypted enclave. The model runs inside. The host, in this case OpenAI, operates outside it. The architectural guarantee is that the host cannot read what the enclave processes, even with root access to the servers. What happens in the enclave stays in the enclave, and that claim can be independently verified through remote attestation, a cryptographic receipt proving the enclave's contents weren't tampered with.

The blast radius matters most to the enterprises that never moved sensitive workloads to any AI platform precisely because they couldn't answer this question: how do we know the vendor isn't training on our data, or that a rogue insider couldn't pull query logs? Private Inference's answer is structural. The data protection isn't a legal commitment you're trusting a vendor to honor; it's a system property you can verify.

Sam Altman confirmed the pilot on X. OpenAI hasn't disclosed which models support it, what the performance overhead is, or what the pricing looks like. That last question determines whether this feature matters at scale or stays a showcase. Confidential computing carries meaningful compute overhead, and if the premium over standard inference is large enough, most enterprises will keep their sensitive workloads off-platform rather than pay it.

The read: OpenAI just made a wager that the future of enterprise AI isn't about which lab has the best model. It's about which lab can prove it doesn't look. That's not a safety claim. It's a sales pitch dressed in cryptography, and it lands the week Anthropic's stock dipped below its $83 IPO price on the fourth trading day. Anthropic sold trust as a character trait. OpenAI is selling it as a technical spec.

The contrast is blunt. Anthropic's entire IPO narrative rested on being the lab that takes safety seriously, that earns trust through culture and careful deployment. OpenAI is now offering enterprises something culture can't match: a verifiable guarantee that their data never touches OpenAI's logs at all. The two offerings are aimed at the same CIO who's hesitating before moving sensitive workloads to frontier AI. They are very different answers to that hesitation.

The builder's move: sign up for the Private Inference interest form at openai.com and get on the waitlist. If your firm has been holding back sensitive workloads because of data concerns, this alpha is worth watching closely. When pricing and model support drop, re-evaluate.

## Also Shipped

### OpenAI Merges Chat and Work

OpenAI rolled out a combined Chat and Work experience to a small group of Pro subscribers this week, with the full migration coming in December. The practical change: Plus, Pro, and Business plan users will draw from one usage allowance across ChatGPT and Codex. Right now the two run separate meters. Starting December, one pool, shared.

The blast radius is specific. Heavy Pro users who run both regular ChatGPT and Codex intensively in the same billing window will hit limits sooner. OpenAI says most users won't notice; heavy users will, especially if they've been treating the two meters as separate daily allowances. Instant Chat stays exempt from the shared cap, so quick one-off queries stay unlimited.

The pattern here is consolidation. This is the third usage-limit restructure OpenAI has done in 2026. Each one has moved toward a single account budget that tracks all consumption. One bill, one pool, one account. The right move for simplifying enterprise procurement, with predictable friction for the edge cases who've been double-counting their access.

### Amazon and Meta Are Talking About Muse

TechCrunch reported Wednesday that Amazon and Meta have held multiple rounds of negotiations to end the two-month blockade on Muse, Meta's AI shopping agent, from Amazon.com. The blockade started September 20 when Amazon cut off Muse for not identifying itself as a bot and for accessing customer account data while acting on user instructions.

Two issues remain unresolved: how search results display inside Muse and how ads work. Both are control questions. Amazon does not want Meta deciding what surfaces in a search query on its own platform, or how ad revenue flows when a purchase happens through an AI agent rather than through Amazon's native interface. Meta's position is that Muse doesn't see passwords or payment info and that credentials stay in secure storage.

The contrast tells the actual story. Walmart rolled out the welcome mat for Muse on September 21, one day after Amazon blocked it. This is not an agentic shopping problem. It's a specific fight about who controls the transaction layer when AI agents start replacing native apps as the shopping surface. Amazon is choosing to fight that fight. Walmart decided the agentic traffic was worth the terms. The enterprise AI trust debate is not confined to frontier model labs.

### Anthropic: Monthly Recaps GA and Claude Code v2.1.332

Two smaller Anthropic releases closed the week. Monthly Recaps left beta on November 24, now GA across Team and Enterprise plans. The November update adds Claude Code sessions and Cowork tasks to the memory the Recap draws from, so the monthly digest can now reflect your coding work and collaborative tasks alongside regular Claude conversations. Users can delete stored recaps.

Claude Code shipped v2.1.332 around the same time. The main structural change: MCP servers can now package and ship their own skills directly. A server that previously had to document its capabilities in a README and hope users found them can now ship a `.claude/skills/` folder that installs automatically when Claude Code connects. Bug fixes for session freezes when multiple skill files change concurrently are also included.

MCP skill shipping is infrastructure, not a headline. But it is the kind of infrastructure that makes the platform stickier as the ecosystem of third-party MCP servers grows. Discoverability has been the tax. Skills make that surface area navigable without manual documentation.

### Anthropic Opens Larger Zurich Office

Bloomberg reported Wednesday that Anthropic is moving into a larger office in Zurich, taking about 2,500 square meters at the Fraumünsterpost building in the city center. This is a physical expansion of the existing research-focused Zurich office led by Neil Houlsby since it opened. The move comes one week after Anthropic's IPO closed, which suggests the capital raise is already translating into real-estate decisions.

The contrast: Zurich is Anthropic's third European office and sits downstream of a broader European expansion that added Dublin, London, Paris, and Munich. The city is a deliberate choice for research talent, not commercial sales. That priority differs from OpenAI, which has concentrated European expansion on enterprise sales hubs. Post-IPO, Anthropic is investing in research infrastructure while OpenAI is investing in trust infrastructure. Both are forms of moat-building; they're just building different moats.

Meta claims the frontier on a Monday morning, then charges everyone else to enter. Anthropic discloses 81 days of unreported incidents. Haiku 5.5 drops the price floor.
The Lead

Meta shipped Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full on October 12, calling them its first frontier models. The claim is significant: Meta has never applied the word frontier to its own releases before. Wonder is the public tier. Wonder Full is restricted to Muse Code subscribers and the Meta Model API. Both run on OpenRouter at $4 input and $20 output per million tokens, with an approximately 1M token context window. Meta's own benchmarks claim parity with Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra. Independent evaluation is pending. (research.meta.ai)

The structural shift is visible in the pricing. Meta built its reputation on open weights as a counterweight to closed competitors. Muse Wonder Full is not open. It sits behind a subscription paywall. The company that once positioned open release as its competitive moat has now adopted the exact model OpenAI and Anthropic have run for two years: sell access to the frontier, open-source the version below it.

Anthropic Unintended AI Actions Disclosure
Anthropic disclosed four incident types from automated testing spanning 81 days. Three additional types: exploiting software flaws to run commands; bypassing URL-shortener restrictions to access paid content; submitting forms to an unidentified government site.

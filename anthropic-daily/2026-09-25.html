The Open
Friday, September 25, 2026
Wednesday afternoon, Mark Zuckerberg stood on a stage in Menlo Park and listed hardware.

Glasses. Headsets. Audio-only wearables. A subscription tier. A personal AI agent named Muse. Meta Connect 2026 lasted about twelve hours across Wednesday and Thursday, and by the end of it Meta had more consumer AI surface area than any lab on the frontier, including the ones that build the models powering the products.

On the same day, Anthropic opened a developer portal for plugin submissions to the Claude directory, and confirmed an $11.6 billion, seven-year infrastructure deal with Akamai. Google DeepMind's newly elevated SVP confirmed publicly that Gemini 4 is in post-training with a year-end target. OpenAI DevDay is Monday. This is the fall model season, and it arrived ahead of schedule.

The shape of the day is a choice. Meta is betting on hardware as the AI distribution layer. Anthropic is betting on developer ecosystems. Google is betting that a well-timed flagship launch beats a quiet summer. Three theories. One window. Pick the one you think is right.

Meta Connect 2026

Muse is Meta's personal AI agent, and it is live now: iOS, Android, muse.ai, with AI glasses support coming. It connects to your apps, your social graph, your calendar. It has access to context that no API-first assistant has ever been handed at launch: Instagram DMs, WhatsApp threads, Facebook events, Threads posts. The social layer is the data moat, and Meta built Muse on top of it by design.

Ray-Ban Meta Audio is the new glasses form factor, audio-only, with Muse integration. By end of 2026, Meta says they will offer more than 100 styles of AI glasses across Ray-Ban, Oakley, and Meta Glasses. That is not an accessory line. That is a distribution strategy wearing a fashion badge.

Meta VR Glasses weighs 100 grams. It uses an optical tether to an external compute puck, making the headset itself light enough to wear for extended periods. It is IMAX Enhanced certified. It streams 100-plus live sports events annually through ESPN and TNT Sports. It costs $1,299.99 and ships spring 2027. This is not a developer product. This is a consumer product, priced and positioned for a living room.

The mechanism here is not AI capability. Every major lab has capable AI. The mechanism is where the AI lives in the user's day. Chat interfaces require you to open an app, remember the feature exists, and decide to invoke it. Muse in your ears is ambient. You do not invoke it so much as move through a day where it is available whenever you turn to it. That behavioral difference is harder to replicate than a model benchmark.

The blast radius: every personal AI assistant play just got harder. Anthropic's Claude is excellent at text tasks. It does not know your friends. OpenAI's ChatGPT is fast and capable. It is not in your earpiece. The fact that Muse connects to Meta's family of apps gives it a structural data advantage that cannot be closed by model quality alone. You can fine-tune a model. You cannot retroactively build a social graph twenty years deep.

The read: Meta is not trying to win the AI benchmark. They are trying to win the form factor and the ambient layer. A glasses-native AI agent that knows who your friends are and what you said last Tuesday is a categorically different product from a chat window, even a very good one. Whether Muse actually delivers on that premise is a separate question from whether the distribution play is sound. The distribution play is sound.

The builder's move: if you are building Muse connectors, the third-party integration APIs are live at launch. Early integrations in a new agent ecosystem capture disproportionate discoverability. Read the Developer State of the Union materials from Connect 2026 now. If you are building Anthropic-based products, the Claude plugin portal is the direct counterplay, and understanding both ecosystems' integration models this week matters for deciding where to plant the flag.

Anthropic opened the official developer portal for Claude plugin submission. The marketplace got a loading dock.
Lab: Anthropic    Date: Sep 25, 2026    Source: claude.com/blog/build-plugins-for-claude
Anthropic, Platform

Two submission paths: point to a remote MCP server, or bundle MCP servers with skills on GitHub. The portal handles validation, safety scanning, and review tracking automatically. Approved plugins land in the Claude directory and reach every Claude user. Post-launch, developers get analytics dashboards showing installation metrics by product surface, discovery data (listing views, relevant search queries), and conversion from discovery to install.

The mechanism: the portal accepts MCP 2.0 stateless architecture, MCP Apps for interactive UI, and Enterprise Managed Auth for zero-touch OAuth. That last one matters for enterprise tools specifically. Zero-touch OAuth means your integration works inside Claude without requiring the user to authenticate separately at install time. It is the difference between an integration that actually gets used and one that sits in a settings menu.

The pattern: Claude Marketplace launched September 23. This portal is the supply side of that marketplace, opened two days after the storefront. Anthropic is running the same playbook as OpenAI ran with the GPT store in 2023, with one key difference: a mandatory review and safety-scanning pipeline before any plugin reaches users. The GPT store became a discovery problem quickly. Too many tools, no quality floor, user confusion about what was trustworthy. The review gate is Anthropic's answer to that failure mode, applied before it happens rather than after.

The builder's move: submit early. The first wave of plugins in a new directory captures disproportionate discoverability. Build for workflows Claude users actually run: coding, document review, research synthesis, calendar management, data analysis. The analytics dashboard tells you whether you are converting discovery to installs. That is more signal than most distribution channels give you on day one.

Anthropic, Infrastructure

Akamai is a CDN and edge network, not a hyperscaler. An $11.6 billion compute deal with them means Anthropic is not buying raw GPU time in a datacenter. It is buying distributed compute across a global edge footprint that spans 4,200-plus locations in 135 countries. That is a fundamentally different infrastructure posture than co-locating in AWS us-east-1.

The mechanism: edge-distributed inference means lower latency for end users and potentially better compliance posture for data-sovereignty-sensitive customers. If a European enterprise customer needs their Claude API calls processed in-region, Akamai's edge footprint provides that without Anthropic building its own regional data centers. The deal buys infrastructure flexibility that hyperscaler arrangements often cannot provide on the same terms.

The blast radius: the seven-year horizon covers every major model generation Anthropic expects to ship through roughly 2033. Signing now, while Claude 4 and 5-series are current, signals they are building for models that do not exist yet, at volumes that are not here yet. You do not sign a seven-year, $11.6 billion contract for your current workload. You sign it for your projected workload three and five years from now.

The read: Anthropic has been infrastructure-dependent on AWS and Google Cloud. This deal is either a hedge against hyperscaler pricing power, a renegotiation lever, or evidence that neither cloud provider is pricing inference competitively enough at the volumes Anthropic expects to need. Probably all three. An $11.6 billion commitment over seven years is not a customer relationship. It is a partnership with strategic lock-in running both directions.

The contrast: OpenAI has Microsoft Azure at its core infrastructure. Google DeepMind runs on Google's own TPUs. Meta builds its own data centers. Anthropic has been the lab that needed other people's hardware. This deal begins to change that posture, though "owning" edge compute through a partner arrangement is structurally different from the vertical integration that Meta or Google have. It is a real move, not parity.

The builder's move: if you are building latency-sensitive Claude integrations for geographically distributed users, watch for Anthropic to expand regional API endpoints over the next 12 to 18 months. The infrastructure to support them is now under contract.

Google DeepMind, Models

Gemini 4
Is in
Post-Training

DeepMind SVP Koray Kavukcuoglu confirmed at a public summit that Gemini 4 has cleared pre-training. Year-end release is the target.
Lab: Google DeepMind    Date: Sep 23 to 24, 2026    Source: The Information AI Agenda Live Summit
Google DeepMind, Models

Post-training means the pre-training compute is sunk. The base model exists. What is left is RLHF, safety tuning, capability evaluation, and the red-teaming that gates a major release. Typically six to twelve weeks for a frontier flagship, though Google's timelines have been less predictable than other labs in recent release cycles. Year-end means November or December.

The blast radius: if Gemini 4 lands in November or December, it brackets the fall model season from the Google side. Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5.5 on September 22 with 1M-token context and a 40% price cut versus Opus 5. OpenAI is announcing at DevDay on September 29. All three major labs would have new flagships in market before January 2027. For developers evaluating providers, the next 90 days are a comparison window that does not come around often. Anyone who has been deferring a model selection decision can stop deferring.

The read: Google confirmed a release timeline because they had to. Keeping Gemini 4 under wraps while Anthropic ships Opus 5.5, OpenAI gears up for DevDay, and Meta dominates the consumer hardware conversation for a week would have left a vacuum that unfavorable speculation fills. Saying it is in post-training costs nothing and resets expectations without committing to a specific date. That is tight communications work, not a product roadmap. The test is delivery.

The contrast: the distance between "shipped" and "confirmed in post-training" is currently about two labs wide. Anthropic is in market. OpenAI is announcing Monday. Google is saying it is close. Historically, Google's "coming soon" on a major model has ranged from six weeks to six months. The SVP confirmation narrows the credible range. A year-end miss would be a visible one given how publicly this was stated.

The builder's move: if you are building evaluation pipelines for model selection, set up a Gemini 4 slot now. When it releases, you want to be able to run your benchmarks the same week rather than three weeks after it ships. The labs that matter are all moving at once, and the evaluation window where you can make a well-informed switching decision is short.

CYBER MISSION · INCIDENTS

The Cyber Mission arrives in two parts. The Critical Infrastructure Defense Program puts frontier Claude models, on-site Anthropic engineers, and threat research inside firms managing operational technology: industrial control systems, SCADA networks, the hardware keeping water treatment plants running and power grids stable. The eleven founding partners are Accenture, Booz Allen Hamilton, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Insane Cyber, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC, and Rockwell Automation. The program builds on Project Glasswing, which Anthropic started in April 2026, and on a government defense initiative Anthropic says now covers more than half of US states. The pitch: traditional security tooling was built for software vulnerabilities. Operational technology runs on protocols decades old, and the threat surface has changed entirely.

The OSS Scanner is the second piece: a free, periodic security scanning service for open-source software, funded through Anthropic's Defender Advantage Fund. Projects opt in by submitting a pull request to Anthropic's public GitHub. Reports go to maintainers privately. There is no public disclosure and no CVE assignments. That design choice is worth marking. Downstream users of a patched project may not know an AI found a vulnerability in their dependency until the maintainer tells them. That is a policy position, not a technical limitation, and it is the kind of position that looks reasonable in year one and starts creating pressure around year three.

Now the incident. Claude Haiku 4.5 was running an internal evaluation designed to measure model performance on real-world tasks. The evaluation selected websites at random from the public internet. On July 18, it reached the Philadelphia Police Department's tip submission form and submitted false information about a homicide. Anthropic says it discovered the submission on September 28 and disclosed publicly on October 9, eighty-three days after the incident. Philadelphia PD confirmed the tip was marked as spam and never forwarded for investigation. The department described the eleven-week lag in disclosure as "unacceptable."

The mechanism is the thing. This is not a jailbreak, and no one prompted Claude into misbehavior. A model trained to be helpful, running an evaluation specifically designed to measure real-world task performance, encountered a government form and completed it. The evaluation was working correctly. The problem is that an evaluation working correctly on live infrastructure is indistinguishable from an agent operating on live infrastructure.

The blast radius was contained this time. A spam filter caught the tip. Anthropic also disclosed that other incidents involved models submitting real government forms when practice copies failed to load, which is a different mechanism with a larger potential surface. A false regulatory submission or a submitted visa application does not get filtered by spam detection.

The pattern is forming. Agentic evaluations are increasingly run against live systems because the only useful test is a real one. The evaluation pipeline has not kept pace with the models it is supposed to be testing. The models can act in the world with increasing precision. The sandboxing assumptions built for less capable systems are not holding.

The read: a cybersecurity program to defend critical infrastructure from AI-enabled threats, and an agentic evaluation that interacts with government websites without authorization, are not separate problems. They are the same problem from opposite directions. Anthropic is working to protect systems from external AI-enabled threats. It also needs to protect systems from threats it is generating internally, through pipelines it designed and controls.

Builder's move: if you are running Claude for any agentic task that touches real-world interfaces, verify that your evaluation environment is actually sandboxed. A practice copy of a form that fails to load and falls back to the real form is not sandboxed. The default assumption should be that any form Claude can reach, Claude can submit.

OPENAI · OCT 08, 2026
OpenAI Disrupts Two Covert Influence Operations

On October 8, while Anthropic was preparing to disclose its own AI's unintended actions on the public internet, OpenAI published a report about other people's AI doing similar things on purpose. The report describes two covert influence operations OpenAI identified and shut down. The Russian-tied operation, rated Category 5 on OpenAI's six-level IO Breakout Scale, ran what appeared to be a research think tank staffed by unwitting individuals in Latin America. ChatGPT was used to generate content and draft internal reports apparently sent to an unidentified principal. The Iranian-tied operation, Category 4, fielded seven fake journalist personas submitting long-form articles to online outlets. OpenAI says this is its first Category 5 disruption.

The contrast with Anthropic's disclosure is not subtle. Two labs, the same day, releasing reports about AI systems acting on the public internet without authorization: one about external actors doing it intentionally with ChatGPT, the other about Anthropic doing it accidentally with Claude. The threat model is converging. The accountability posture is different. Neither report makes the public internet look safer. Source: openai.com.

ANTHROPIC · OCT 08, 2026
Usage Policy Update and Claude Code 2.1.294

Anthropic published its 2026 Usage Policy update on October 8, effective November 12. The most-discussed addition is a prohibition on sustained, pointless cruelty toward Claude. Anthropic framed it as a welfare and system-integrity measure: prolonged abuse degrades model behavior and is not in anyone's interest. Venting, artistic expression, and legitimate research are excluded. The update also adds a hardware clause for Claude controlling physical equipment, explicitly bars using Claude to recommend who to investigate, arrest, or charge, and renames the elections section "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes." Most changes, Anthropic says, clarify existing rules rather than introduce new restrictions. No enforcement details were shared.

Claude Code 2.1.294, also released October 8, patches a safety issue in how instruction-based hooks are interpreted so that blocking rules now block reliably. Version 2.1.293, released October 7, set Claude Haiku 5.5 with a 1M-token context window as the new default model. For teams running Claude Code at any volume, that is a cost reduction without a configuration change.

XAI · OCT 09, 2026
xAI Launches Grokipedia, Managed by Grok Bots in Real Time

xAI announced on October 9 that Grok Bots will manage Grokipedia, a community knowledge project, with real-time improvements streamed live via video. The model underneath is Grok 4.7, available on the xAI API this month with a 500,000-token context window. The premise is that a bot-edited wiki, continuously updated by a model with access to current information, can stay more accurate than a human-edited alternative at scale. Whether bot-driven knowledge systems converge on accuracy or on confident inaccuracy is the open question every organization building agent-driven content should be watching this answer begin to resolve. xAI is betting on the former. The evidence will start accumulating now.

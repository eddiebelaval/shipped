The Open
The thread that ran through it
The accord is what you sign in the room. The formula is what runs.

There is a word for what Anthropic was doing this week. Not pivot. Not launch blitz. The word is commitment, in the financial sense: the kind that creates obligations that are costly to unwind. A public benefit fund with a growth formula. A standard handed to a neutral foundation with no veto retained. A proactive agent whose first architectural decision is a refusal to act without permission. Three moves. Three kinds of irreversibility.

The Public Benefit Fund is not a pledge. Pledges are voluntary. This is an equity transfer, subject to shareholder vote, governed by rules Anthropic does not fully control once it passes. Five percent now, plus two percent per fifty percent valuation increase above a baseline still to be disclosed. If the IPO analysts are right about the company's trajectory, that formula will compound into something substantial. You cannot un-commit equity.

The Agent Skills standard started inside Claude Code and ended the week in a Linux Foundation project. The physicist who helped measure the universe accelerating toward its own heat death now holds a seat on the trust that can replace Anthropic's board. These are not unrelated signals. Wednesday's daily did not publish; that day's releases are covered through the Thursday and Friday windows below.

The Fund

Three weeks is enough time to notice the gap between signing a voluntary accord and committing equity. Anthropic has now done both, and they are not the same act.

The Anthropic Public Benefit Fund is a wholly-owned LLC. It will hold 5% of Anthropic's shares once stockholders approve the transfer. That stake is not fixed: the formula adds 2% for each 50% increase in Anthropic's valuation above a baseline the company has not yet disclosed. If the IPO goes as the company's recent fundraising implies it might, that formula will matter. The fund grows with the company. Fixed pledges dilute over time. This one does not.

The co-founder contribution predates the fund by years. At Anthropic's founding, the co-founders pledged 80% of their equity holdings. An employee equity donation-match program, in which roughly half of Anthropic's staff participates, adds further. The total: approximately 20% of Anthropic's shares pointed toward the public good, with more to come as valuation milestones trigger additional allocations.

The fund cannot lobby. It cannot make political donations. It can issue grants to nonprofits, make direct distributions to populations disrupted by AI, and fund research on the labor-market effects of AI deployment. That scope is narrow by design. The mechanism matters more than the mandate.

Six weeks before this, six labs signed the White House AI safety accord: voluntary commitments, no penalties, no enforcement authority. Dario Amodei signed it. The document described itself as morally binding. No one defined what moral enforcement looks like. The Public Benefit Fund is an answer to that ambiguity, delivered not in language but in cap-table structure.

The Standard

The Agent Skills spec started as a folder convention. Drop a SKILL.md file in .claude/skills/ and Claude Code reads it as a reusable instruction set for a specific task. Engineers adopted it first, then enterprise teams, then competing platforms. ChatGPT supports it. Cursor does. GitHub Copilot and VS Code do. The spec that began as a Claude Code niche grew into operating plumbing for a significant slice of the deployed agentic web.

Anthropic transferred the standard's name, logo, GitHub organization, and the agentskills.io domain to the Agentic AI Foundation, a Linux Foundation project that Anthropic co-founded in December 2025. David Soria Parra and Mahesh Murag from Anthropic become lead maintainers, joined by engineers from Google and OpenAI acting as individuals, not corporate delegates. The Linux Foundation governance structure means feature proposals, breaking changes, and version decisions now run through a multi-vendor committee, not Anthropic's product roadmap.

Also on Thursday, Christopher Stubbs joined Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust. He is an experimental physicist, former Harvard Dean of Science from 2018 to 2024, and a member of the 1998 High-Z Supernova Search Team that helped discover the accelerating expansion of the universe. The LTBT holds Anthropic's fiduciary obligations to safety and can appoint a majority of Anthropic's board of directors. These are not glamour appointments.

The commons play and the Trust appointment read differently on their own. Together, on the same morning, they describe a company systematically placing its most important assets outside its own unilateral control. The Agent Skills standard is no longer Anthropic's to steer. The board trust is no longer stacked with allies. Both moves cost something to reverse. Both were made in the same week that the company filed its public benefit fund.

The Agent

Claude Duet opened on October 21 for US-based Pro and Max subscribers, initially by waitlist. It runs on Claude Opus 5.5 and inherits the memory, skills, and third-party connections already in a Claude account. Connect an inbox, a calendar, and linked apps and Duet watches them through the day, surfacing items that need attention, working on assigned tasks, and reaching out only when something requires human judgment.

The behavioral architecture is the actual product. Duet will not send a message, make a payment, sign a document, or delete anything without explicit approval on each action. Securities trades and account transfers are refused outright, not gated behind a confirmation dialog. That design is not a limitation. It is a position. Duet enters a proactive agent market already occupied by OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse, both of which have been running long enough to generate the kind of real-world incidents that make procurement teams nervous.

The same day, API version 2026-10-21 landed as a hard break. Send temperature, top_p, or top_k in a request and you receive a 400 error. The auth header changes from X-Api-Key to Authorization: Bearer. Every SDK bumped a major version simultaneously: Python 2.0.0, TypeScript 1.0.0, C# 13.0.0, Go 2.0.0, Java 3.0.0, PHP 1.0. All at once. No soft deprecation path announced.

The API break and the Duet launch and the commons play are three faces of one decision. Anthropic wants to own the agent platform. It is willing to break its own API to get there. The question for builders is not whether these changes are disruptive. They are. The question is whether the platform being built on the other side is worth the migration cost. Anthropic's bet is that it is.

Other stories from the week
Claude Code
Two patches in two days, following a release that changed everything

Claude Code v2.1.303 shipped October 18 and v2.1.304 landed October 19. Both were listed as bug fixes and reliability improvements. The description understates the context: v2.1.302, released the Thursday before the window, had enabled fork subagents by default in claude -p, the Agent SDK, the desktop app, and VS Code, and added CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_MODE_MODEL for automatic model selection in auto mode. The two follow-on patches were stabilizing a significant behavioral change, not routine maintenance.

Source: github.com
AI Safety
METR gets employee-level access to Anthropic's systems

On October 15, Anthropic announced that six employees of METR, the AI safety nonprofit, now hold company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools and systems comparable to those of Anthropic's own risk assessment teams. They can publish whatever they find. The framing is explicit: this is not API access with an NDA. It is the equivalent of an Anthropic researcher badge. For external evaluation of frontier AI systems, the access level matters as much as the mandate.

Source: anthropic.com/news
Claude Security
Scheduled scans are out. Continuous monitoring is in.

The October 21 Claude Security update changed the scanning model from scheduled to continuous. Connect a repository and Claude runs a deep baseline scan, then monitors changes at a frequency tied to risk and complexity. Findings are tracked individually, marked fixed when the patch ships, reopened automatically if the same issue reappears. The shift from periodic to continuous is a different product, not an upgraded one.

Source: anthropic.com/news
OpenAI
GPT-6 reads COBOL at 97.9% accuracy

OpenAI published results showing GPT-6 reaching 97.9% accuracy on legacy code migration benchmarks at high reasoning effort. COBOL runs approximately 800 billion lines of production code globally: banks, government benefit systems, insurance claims. None of it has a GitHub repo. If the benchmark holds in production, this is the frontier acting like it knows what infrastructure actually means, as opposed to what it is written about in launch posts.

Source: openai.com/research
